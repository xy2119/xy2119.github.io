MoCoTalk: Multi-Conditional Diffusion with Adaptive Router for Controllable Talking Head Generation

Xinyan Ye, Jiankang Deng, Abbas Edalat
Imperial College London
Paper arXiv Code

Video-driven methods follow the driving video but ignore speech; audio-driven methods follow speech but give little control over pose or lighting. MoCoTalk takes all of them as conditions at once, with independent control of facial expression, head pose and mouth dynamics.

Abstract

Talking-head generation requires joint modeling of identity, head pose, facial expression, and mouth dynamics. Existing methods typically address only a subset of these factors, and rely on fixed-weight or heuristic fusion when multiple conditions are involved. We present MoCoTalk, a multi-conditional video diffusion framework that unifies four complementary control signals: a reference image, facial keypoints, 3DMM-rendered shading meshes, and the corresponding speech audio.

To resolve destructive interference among heterogeneous conditions, we introduce an Adaptive Multi-Condition Router that computes channel-wise, timestep-aware gating over the four condition streams, allowing the fusion strategy to vary with both feature subspace and noise level. To better capture speech-related facial dynamics, we design a Mouth-Augmented Shading Mesh, a 3DMM-based representation that decouples head motion, mouth motion, expression, and lighting. This design provides a temporally consistent geometric prior and allows flexible recombination of these attributes at inference. We further introduce a lip consistency loss to tighten audio-visual alignment.

Extensive experiments show that MoCoTalk achieves state-of-the-art performance on the majority of structural, motion, and perceptual metrics, while offering attribute-level controllability that single-condition methods do not provide.

Method Overview

MoCoTalk accepts four complementary conditioning signals: a reference portrait, facial keypoints, mouth-augmented 3DMM shading meshes and speech audio. Lighting, shape, pose and expression are extracted from video frames with DECA and SPECTRE and rendered into the shading mesh; the audio is encoded by Wav2Vec2 into a windowed feature sequence. Four parallel adapters lift the signals into spatially aligned latent maps, and the router fuses them into the shallow U-Net feature through channel-wise gating. The fused feature guides a Stable Video Diffusion backbone to denoise the video latents.

1

Adaptive Multi-Condition Router

Pools each condition, the U-Net feature and the timestep embedding, then predicts a softmax gate per channel over the four branches:

h = hunet + Σk gk ⊙ ck

Missing conditions are masked out, so any subset of conditions works at inference.

2

Mouth-Augmented Shading Mesh

Identity and lighting from DECA, mouth and expression from SPECTRE plus a DECA residual, head pose from a separate video. Head motion, mouth motion, expression and lighting can each come from a different source.

3

Lip Consistency Loss

A 1 − cos loss between frozen lip-reading features of predicted and ground-truth mouth crops, which tightens audio-visual alignment of the mouth region.

Mouth-Augmented Shading Mesh

Fitting DECA to each frame on its own produces weak, jittery mouth shapes. Replacing the mouth-region coefficients with those recovered by SPECTRE, while keeping DECA's global head motion, gives a mesh that follows the speech closely and stays stable over time.

  • The router matters most. Replacing it with fixed additive fusion raises FVD about 14× (40.75 → 592.5) and lowers ID by 18–20% in both the self and cross settings.
  • The lip loss improves motion accuracy (AED, APD, AKD) and cross-identity transfer.
